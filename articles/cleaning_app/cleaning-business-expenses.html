12 Cleaning Business Expenses to Track Every Month

By Constance Beebe, former hotel general manager with more than 20 years in hospitality

Most cleaning businesses don’t lose money on one big expense. They lose it on small ones that never get written down. Tracking your cleaning business expenses every month shows what each job really costs, helps you price with confidence, and turns tax season into a quick review instead of a scramble.

From my experience

As an executive housekeeper and later a general manager, I learned that budgets rarely slip on one big purchase. They slip on the small things that add up: another case of product, extra linens, a repair nobody wrote down. Tracking those costs every month is how you catch them early, while they’re still easy to fix.

12 cleaning business expenses to track

  1. Cleaning products. All-purpose, glass, bathroom, and floor cleaners, plus disinfectants.
  2. Reusable supplies. Microfiber cloths, mop pads, scrub pads, brushes, and spray bottles.
  3. Equipment purchases and repairs. Vacuums, mop systems, and the parts and filters that keep them running.
  4. Gas, mileage, and vehicle costs. Keep a mileage log for every trip between jobs.
  5. Parking and tolls. Small amounts that add up quickly in busy areas.
  6. Business insurance. General liability and any bonding or other coverage you carry.
  7. Licenses, permits, and fees. Business registration, local licenses, and renewal fees.
  8. Wages and payroll costs. Pay for helpers or employees and the employer costs that come with it.
  9. Phone and internet. The business share of your phone plan and home internet.
  10. Software and apps. Scheduling, invoicing, bookkeeping, and website subscriptions.
  11. Advertising and marketing. Business cards, flyers, online ads, and directory listings.
  12. Bank and payment processing fees. Card fees, payment app fees, and account charges.

How to track cleaning business expenses

A simple routine works better than a complicated system you never keep up with:

  • Use a separate bank account or card for business purchases.
  • Save every receipt, on paper or as a photo, in a folder for each month.
  • Record each expense in the same categories every time, like the twelve above.
  • Set aside fifteen minutes each week to enter receipts and mileage.
  • At the end of each month, total each category and compare it with last month.

One-time purchases and monthly costs

Separate one-time purchases, such as a new vacuum, from the costs you pay every month, such as software and insurance. Monthly costs belong in every price you quote. One-time purchases are better spread over the months or years you expect the item to last. If you are just getting started, our guide to what it really costs to start a small cleaning business covers the first purchases to plan for.

Put your expense records to work

Once you have a few months of records, you can see which costs are creeping up, how much each job really costs to deliver, and whether your prices still cover everything. That is the information you need to raise your cleaning prices with confidence when the time comes.

Questions cleaners ask

How often should I record my cleaning business expenses?

Weekly is ideal. A short weekly routine keeps receipts from piling up, and a monthly total shows you how each category is trending.

Should I track mileage or actual vehicle costs?

Keep a mileage log either way. In the U.S., there are two ways to deduct vehicle costs for business, a standard mileage rate or actual expenses, so ask your tax professional which one fits your situation.

Do I need bookkeeping software?

Not necessarily. A spreadsheet or app that uses the same categories every month is enough for most small cleaning businesses, as long as you keep it up to date.
