Rental Property Records to Keep All Year

Good rental records are built a little at a time, not in a rush every spring. Whether a property manager handles your bookings or you do it yourself, keeping these records organized by property and by month makes year-end simple and gives you a clear picture of how each property performs.

9 records to keep all year

  1. Owner statements from your property manager or rental program, every month.
  2. Rental income by month, recorded before fees, with fees recorded separately.
  3. Every expense, by category, such as management, cleaning, repairs, supplies, utilities, insurance, and HOA dues.
  4. Repair and maintenance invoices, especially for larger work.
  5. Property tax and insurance bills, including amounts paid through a mortgage escrow account.
  6. Mortgage statements showing interest and principal separately.
  7. A log of owner stays and other personal use. For vacation homes, personal-use days can affect how expenses are treated, so keep the dates.
  8. Major improvements and their costs, such as a new roof or HVAC system, which are generally depreciated rather than deducted all at once.
  9. A year-end summary of income and expenses to share with your tax professional.

Keep the original receipts and statements, on paper or as saved files, in a folder for each year. A simple monthly habit of entering the month’s numbers and filing the paperwork turns tax season into a quick review instead of a search.

The Rental Property Finances app keeps each property’s income and expenses by category, with a receipt-reference note on every entry so you can find the original paperwork.
