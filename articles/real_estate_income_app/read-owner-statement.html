How to Read Your Property Manager’s Owner Statement

If a property manager or resort rental program runs your home, the monthly owner statement is your main window into how the property is doing. It arrives, the payout lands, and it is easy to glance at the bottom line and move on. A few minutes with each statement tells you far more: what the home actually earned, what it cost to run, and whether anything needs a question.

8 lines to check on every owner statement

  1. Gross rental revenue. Compare the nights booked and the rates charged with your own booking calendar.
  2. Management commission. Confirm the percentage matches your agreement, and note whether it is calculated on gross revenue or on revenue after certain fees.
  3. Cleaning and turnover charges. See whether guests or you paid them, and how many cleans were billed compared with the number of stays.
  4. Lodging or accommodations taxes. Many programs collect and remit these for you. Make sure they are shown separately and are not counted as your income.
  5. Booking, channel, or card processing fees. These can add up quietly across a busy season.
  6. Repairs and maintenance. Ask for the invoice behind any charge you don’t recognize, especially larger ones.
  7. Owner stays and blocked dates. Confirm that your own visits are recorded correctly and not billed as guest stays.
  8. Net payout. Match the final amount to the deposit that actually reached your bank account, and note the date.

Keep every statement, even in quiet months. Over a full year, they show your property’s true pattern of income and expenses, which matters for budgeting, for comparing one season with the next, and at tax time.

Record the details, not just the payout

A payout is income after fees. If you record only that number, you lose sight of what your management, cleaning, and repair costs really are. Recording gross revenue and each fee separately shows where your money goes, and makes it easier to notice when one category starts creeping up.

The Rental Property Finances app gives each statement a place to land. Record gross rent and each charge in its own category, such as Management, Cleaning, Repairs, HOA, and Utilities. The app’s monthly actuals then show what each property earned and spent, and you can switch years to compare this season with last.
