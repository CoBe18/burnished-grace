The Real Cost of Owning a Vacation Rental

A busy summer can make a vacation rental look like it pays for itself. The truer picture comes from the whole year: slower seasons, owner stays, repairs, and the fixed bills that arrive whether the home is booked or not. Whether you manage the property yourself or through a rental program, these are the costs to plan for.

10 costs to plan for

  1. Mortgage payment. Track interest and principal separately; they are treated differently in your results and at tax time.
  2. Property taxes. Rental properties may be taxed at a different rate than a primary residence, depending on your location.
  3. Insurance. Include any wind, flood, or rental-use coverage your location and lender require.
  4. HOA, regime, or resort fees. Common in resort communities, and often due whether or not the home is rented.
  5. Management commission. Usually a percentage of rental revenue, plus any separate fees in your agreement.
  6. Cleaning, linens, and turnover. Know which costs guests pay and which come out of your share.
  7. Utilities and services. Electricity, water, internet, streaming, pest control, and pool or landscape care.
  8. Repairs and routine maintenance. Coastal and high-use homes tend to need more frequent attention.
  9. Furnishings and replacements. Linens, small appliances, and furniture wear out faster with guest use.
  10. Reserves. Set money aside for slow seasons and for large items like a roof, HVAC system, or exterior work.

Compare these costs with a full year of income rather than your best months. That shows whether the property truly supports itself, how much cushion you have, and what a quieter season would mean for your budget.
