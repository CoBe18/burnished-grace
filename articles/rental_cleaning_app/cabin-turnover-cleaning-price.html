Pricing a Cabin Turnover by Bedrooms Alone Is How Smoky Mountain Cleaners Lose Money

By Constance Beebe, former hotel general manager with more than 20 years in hospitality

Guests leave a Pigeon Forge cabin at 10 a.m., and the next family has the door code at 4 p.m. In between, someone has to strip and remake every bed, scrub every bathroom, reset the game room, sweep the deck and get the hot tub ready. Price that cabin by bedrooms alone and a 5-bedroom turnover starts at $275, the Smoky Mountains starting price built into our Rental Cleaning Pricing Calculator. In the example on our shop page, the same size cabin comes to $465 once each extra is listed as its own line. The gap between those two numbers is the work most quotes leave out.

This guide is for the independent cleaner in Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge, Sevierville or Townsend who has just been asked by a cabin owner, often one who lives out of state, to take over their turnovers. Here is how to build a price you can defend.

From my experience

I built the Rental Cleaning Pricing Calculator from my years running a Gatlinburg cabin rental company, after two decades in hotel operations. In a hotel, a room is a room. In a cabin, the hot tub, the stone fireplace, the loft full of bunk beds and the drive up the mountain are all part of the job, and each one takes time someone has to pay for.

How much do cleaners charge for a cabin turnover in Gatlinburg and Pigeon Forge?

Published local prices put a small cabin turnover at about $125 to $150 and a 4-bedroom cabin with a hot tub and game room at about $200 to $275. Hero House Cleaning, which serves Gatlinburg, says its standard 1 to 2 bedroom cabin turnover starts at $150, and that luxury lodges of 8 to 12 bedrooms run $300 to $400 or more. A RedAwning guide to hiring cleaners in Sevierville quotes one local rate card at "vacation rental turnover from $125" and gives these ranges for what owners pay the cleaner per turnover:

  • 1 bedroom: $110 to $150
  • 2 bedrooms: $140 to $225
  • 3 bedrooms: $180 to $280
  • 4 bedrooms: $225 to $350
  • 5 to 6 bedrooms: $300 to $450
  • 7 bedrooms and up: quoted after a walkthrough

The calculator's built-in Smoky Mountains prices come from those same two sources and sit at or below the low end of each range: $125 for one bedroom, $175 for three, $275 for five and $425 for eight, plus $45 for each bedroom past eight. Treat them as a floor to start from, not a rule, and change them to what you actually charge.

Why isn't the bedroom count enough to price a turnover?

Because two cabins with the same number of bedrooms can take very different amounts of work. Hero House says its pricing scales with bedrooms, square footage and the number of amenities, and its cabin turnover list shows why: log surfaces that need dry cleaning methods, soot on a stone fireplace, a hot tub area to sanitize, a deck to clear of mountain debris, a game room to reset, and restocking from the owner's supplies. A 4-bedroom cabin with three king beds is a lighter strip-and-make than a 4-bedroom cabin with two bunk rooms, and a cabin at the top of a gravel road costs more drive time than one off the Parkway.

When the base price has to cover all of that, you either overcharge the simple cabins or quietly eat the cost on the busy ones. Owners also argue less with a quote they can read line by line than with one large number.

Which extras should be their own line on a cabin cleaning quote?

Anything that changes the hours or the supplies from one turnover to the next belongs on its own line. These are the ones that come up most in Smoky Mountain cabins:

  • Hot tub service. RedAwning found no published line-item hot tub rate in the area, which is exactly why it gets lost. Agree with the owner whether each turnover gets a test-and-balance or a full drain and clean, and price them separately. The calculator's Smokies starting price for the hot tub extra is $25.
  • Laundry. Decide how many loads the base price covers. RedAwning treats laundry beyond the agreed load count as an upcharge, and recommends owners keep their own linens so quality and losses are easy to check.
  • Same-day rush. RedAwning cites a national norm of 20 to 30 percent extra, or $30 to $50, for a rush. Set your own figure and write it down before the first one comes in.
  • Heavy use. Large groups, pets and parties leave more behind. Name the fee before the first booking so it is not a fight after the worst one.
  • Peak dates. Holidays and fall color weekends stack turnovers on the same day, and you may need to pay extra help to cover them.

Can one cleaner finish a 5-bedroom cabin between checkout and check-in?

Often not, and the clock is the first thing to check before you say yes. RedAwning notes that the area's major operators publish a 10 a.m. checkout and 4 p.m. check-in, a six-hour window, and the Sevierville rate card it quotes estimates two to five hours per cabin clean. A five-hour clean leaves one hour for driving, the hot tub and any surprise mess. One Sevier County operator warns guests that a tub may not be hot for several hours after a full drain and refill, so on drain days the tub has to be done first or the crew has to be bigger.

Work it backward. Estimate the hours the cabin really takes, divide by the people on the crew, add the drive, and see if you finish before check-in with room to spare. If you need a second cleaner to make the window, their pay is part of the price, not a favor you do the owner.

How do you know a turnover price leaves you a profit?

Add up what the job costs before you look at what the market pays. That means crew pay and any payroll costs, cleaning supplies, laundry products, and fuel and wear on your vehicle for the drive up and back. Subtract that from the price, and what is left is your profit. It should also cover your own time spent scheduling, answering owners and buying supplies. Our guide to pricing a cleaning job without forgetting your overhead lists nine costs every quote should cover.

If a cabin only works at a price that leaves nothing, the honest answer is a higher quote or a polite no. Owners who have lost a cleaner in peak season usually understand that a reliable one costs more than a cheap one.

Questions cleaners ask

Should I charge by the hour or by the cabin for vacation rental turnovers?

Most cabin owners want a flat price per turnover, so price by cabin size and list the extras. Hourly billing works better for seasonal deep cleans, where the work is harder to predict.

How much more should a deep clean cost than a regular turnover?

One Smoky Mountains guide suggests about 1.3 to 1.6 times a regular turnover for a routine seasonal deep clean, and 2 to 3 times for cleanup after damage or an incident, while noting that sources disagree. A Sevierville cleaner's published rate card starts deep cleaning at $300.

Should hot tub cleaning be included in the turnover price?

List it as its own line. Testing and adjusting the water is quicker than a full drain and refill, and a refilled tub may not be hot for several hours, so agree with the owner on which service each turnover gets.

Who should supply the linens for a cabin rental?

Many owners keep their own linens so losses are easy to track. If you supply linens or wash more loads than you agreed, charge for it as a separate line.
