A Beatitudes Coloring Book Isn't a Devotional Until the Page Asks You Something

By Constance Beebe, former hotel general manager with more than 20 years in hospitality

On page 6 of our Adult Devotional Edition, a man stands in the temple courtyard with his head bowed and one hand pressed to his chest. Behind him, a Pharisee stands tall, praying with his face lifted. You color the tax collector's robe and the stones under his sandals, and on the facing page the book asks you something: "He stood far off and could only pray, 'God, be merciful to me, a sinner!' What would you bring to God in prayer today?" Below the question are sixteen empty lines. That question is the difference between a pretty afternoon and a quiet time that goes somewhere.

This guide is for the adult who already owns a stack of mandala and garden coloring books, has a morning quiet time that keeps getting squeezed, and wants to spend the coming weeks in Matthew 5. Here is what to look for in a Beatitudes coloring book, and how to use one so it becomes prayer and not just a hobby with a Bible verse on it.

What makes a Beatitudes coloring book a devotional and not just a coloring book?

A devotional gives you something to think about and somewhere to put your answer, and a plain coloring page gives you only the picture. A verse printed across the top of an illustration is a good start, but your hands are busy and your mind wanders. When a question sits beside the page, the wandering has somewhere to go. You color the tax collector, you think about what you would bring to God, and you write it down before the thought slips away.

So when you compare books, look at the page opposite the illustration. In our adult edition, each of the 40 illustrations faces a page with the verse from Matthew 5 (ESV), an "As you color" question, and lined space headed "Notes and prayers." The tax collector's page also sends you to the passage the scene comes from, Luke 18:9–14. Each Beatitude also has its own Reflect page. That is why the book runs 104 pages for 40 pictures.

Why should the pictures show people instead of the hillside where Jesus taught?

Because Jesus spoke each blessing about a kind of person, and Scripture is full of people who fit the description. "Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 5:3) is easier to understand when you are coloring a man who could only say "be merciful to me, a sinner." In the same series, a page for "Blessed are the merciful" (Matthew 5:7) shows the Good Samaritan caring for a wounded man beside his donkey, and a page for those persecuted for righteousness (Matthew 5:10) shows two prisoners in chains looking up toward a window.

A book that only shows a hillside crowd forty times gives you less to think about. A book that pairs each Beatitude with real people and their stories sends you back into your Bible, which is where a devotional should send you.

Which Bible translation should a Beatitudes coloring book use?

Use one close to the Bible you read every day, so the words on the page match the words you already know. Our English editions quote the ESV. Our Spanish editions, Las Bienaventuranzas, quote the Reina-Valera 1909, which helps in a bilingual home or for a Spanish-speaking parent or grandparent who wants the same book in their own language.

How many pages should you color in one sitting?

One is enough. The point is not to finish the book but to stay with one person and one question long enough to answer it honestly. Forty illustrations at one a day is forty days, which fits two seasons of the church year well:

  • Advent. The first Sunday of Advent this year is November 29. Start that week and you will finish the last page in early January, carrying the Beatitudes through Christmas and into the new year.
  • Lent. Ash Wednesday 2027 is February 10. Lent's forty days line up with forty illustrations, one for each day.

If a daily page is too much, take one Beatitude a week: color its pages on the days you can, then sit with its Reflect page on the weekend.

Is a printable coloring book as good as a bound one?

For a devotional, a printable book has real advantages. Ours is a PDF at 8.5 × 11 in, so you print it at home on whatever paper suits your pencils or markers. You can print only the Beatitude you are working on and keep the pages in a folder with your Bible. If you color a page you love, or ruin one, you can print it again, because each edition is for personal and household use. The color cover is a separate, optional file, since it uses a lot of ink.

The trade-off is that you do the printing. If you would rather not, print one Beatitude at a time so a stack of 104 pages never sits waiting on the desk.

Is a Beatitudes coloring book for adults different from one for children?

It should be. A children's book needs bold, simple lines and the verse. An adult devotional needs detailed illustrations, the verse, a question to think about while you color, and room to write your answer.
